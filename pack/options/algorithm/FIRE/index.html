FIRE()

Up: algorithm

FIRE (fast inertial relaxation engine) is a minimization that behaves like a damped oscillator with a memory [Bitzek2006], [Guenole2020]. Each step moves the particles with a semi-implicit Euler step, then mixes the velocity toward the direction of the force:

\[v \leftarrow (1 - \alpha) v + \alpha \, |v| \, \frac{F}{|F|},\]

with $\alpha$ starting at the mixing coefficient. When the power $W = F \cdot v$ (the FIRE papers write $P$, which SoftPack uses for the pressure) is positive the algorithm is going downhill, so the step is allowed to grow and $\alpha$ is shrunk; when the power is negative it is going uphill, so the step is cut and $\alpha$ is reset.

Why it is the default

FIRE therefore needs no tuning of its time step for a new packing, which is why it is the default.

The first ten parameters are the standard FIRE 2.0 scheme, with the LAMMPS values; integrator, displacement_max, damping and drag are SoftPack additions. You rarely need to touch them.

OptionDefaultMeaning
timestep_max10.0upper bound of the adaptive step, in multiples of the starting step
timestep_min0.02lower bound; a run that reaches it is stuck
delay20steps of downhill before the step is allowed to grow
timestep_grow1.1factor applied to the step on a downhill step
timestep_shrink0.5factor applied on an uphill step
mixing_coefficient0.25the starting $\alpha$
mixing_shrink0.99factor applied to $\alpha$ on each downhill step
uphill_max2000uphill steps tolerated before the run stops with :uphill_max
inertia_correctiontruewhether an uphill move steps back half a step (LAMMPS halfstepback)
delay_initialtruewhether the first delay steps are taken before growth
integrator:semi_implicit_eulerthe only integrator FIRE implements
displacement_max0.1the largest displacement of one step, in reference diameters
dampingnothingoptional contact dashpots, see damping
dragnothingoptional Stokes drag, see drag

Checks

The mixing is mass-weighted: the speed is $\sqrt{\sum_i m_i|v_i|^2 + \sum_i I_i|\omega_i|^2}$, the force direction is $F_i/m_i$ and $\tau_i/I_i$, and the power that decides downhill is taken from the elastic forces alone, so a dashpot or drag cannot steer the minimization. With equal masses and no rotation this reduces to the unweighted rule, bit for bit.

FIRE validates its own parameters at the call: timestep_min must not exceed timestep_max, timestep_grow must be at least 1, timestep_shrink must lie in $(0, 1]$, and delay and uphill_max must be positive. With damping set, the step ceiling is clamped to the damped stability limit measured from the packing's stiffest contact. FIRE with friction runs but warns that the combination is non-standard; the rotational mixing is provisional.

Stopping

FIRE stops when the convergence test holds, when steps_max is reached, or when it has spent uphill_max steps without finding a downhill direction. The last case is reported as stop_reason = :uphill_max; it usually means the packing is stuck on a saddle and a restart with a different seed or a looser tolerance is the way forward.

Example

using SoftPack

packing = pack(
    algorithm = FIRE(
        delay = 20,             # downhill steps before growth
        timestep_grow = 1.1,
        mixing_coefficient = 0.25,
    ),
    particles_count = 256,
    seed = 1,
)
packing.converged
