FIRE()

Up: algorithm

FIRE (fast inertial relaxation engine) is a minimization that behaves like a damped oscillator with a memory (design §14). Each step moves the particles with a semi-implicit Euler step, then mixes the velocity toward the direction of the force:

\[v \leftarrow (1 - \alpha) v + \alpha \, |v| \, \frac{F}{|F|},\]

with $\alpha$ starting at the mixing coefficient. When the power $P = F \cdot v$ is positive the algorithm is going downhill, so the step is allowed to grow and $\alpha$ is shrunk; when the power is negative it is going uphill, so the step is cut and $\alpha$ is reset. FIRE therefore needs no tuning of its time step for a new packing, which is why it is the default.

The 11 parameters are the ones of the standard scheme; their defaults are the LAMMPS values. You rarely need to touch them.

OptionDefaultMeaning
timestep_max10.0upper bound of the adaptive step
timestep_min0.02lower bound; a run that reaches it is stuck
delay20steps of downhill before the step is allowed to grow
timestep_grow1.1factor applied to the step on a downhill step
timestep_shrink0.5factor applied on an uphill step
mixing_coefficient0.25the starting $\alpha$
mixing_shrink0.99factor applied to $\alpha$ on each downhill step
uphill_max2000uphill steps tolerated before the run stops with :uphill_max
inertia_correctiontruewhether $\alpha$ follows the standard FIRE schedule
delay_initialtruewhether the first delay steps are taken before growth
integrator:semi_implicit_eulerthe only integrator FIRE implements
dampingnothingoptional contact dashpots, see damping
dragnothingoptional Stokes drag, see drag

Stopping

FIRE stops when the convergence test holds, when steps_max is reached, or when it has spent uphill_max steps without finding a downhill direction. The last case is reported as stop_reason = :uphill_max; it usually means the packing is stuck on a saddle and a restart with a different seed or a looser tolerance is the way forward.
