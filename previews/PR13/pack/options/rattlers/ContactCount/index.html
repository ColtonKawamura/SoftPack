ContactCount()

Up: rattlers

Remove every particle that does not have enough contacts to be held [Xiong2019], [Gilabert2007]. Without friction a particle needs $D + 1$ contacts: three in 2D and four in 3D. With friction two are enough, because a pair of contacts inside the friction cones can hold it.

The rule is applied repeatedly. Each round rebuilds the network without the particles already removed; a neighbor that drops below the threshold goes too, and the rounds stop when a round changes nothing. Removing a particle takes contacts away from its neighbors, so a packing just above its rigidity threshold can lose a chain of particles from one seed.

Why it is the default

This is the default rule because it is the one that matches the isostatic argument: at low pressure the backbone should have exactly the number of contacts that rigidity requires.

The cone test

With friction, a particle with exactly two contacts can only be held if its two contact forces can point into the friction cones. The two contact directions enclose an angle $\theta$, and a pair of forces inside the cones can only balance when

\[\theta \ge \pi - 2\arctan \mu.\]

With $\mu = 0.3$ that is $\theta \ge 146^\circ$: the two contacts must be nearly opposite. A two-contact particle that fails the test is removed, because no force it can transmit would hold it. cone_test = true (the default) applies it.

The cone test runs under every rule except TwoContact(), which removes two-contact particles before the test could matter. It sees only contacts above the rule's force floor, so a contact the rule has already declared broken cannot keep a particle.

The cone test is also where a bug in an earlier version emptied whole frictional packings: the direction from a particle to its contact point is $-n$ when the particle is the first member of the pair and $+n$ when it is the second, and using $n$ for both mixed the angle with its complement.

Options

OptionDefaultMeaning
cone_testtrueapply the friction-cone test to two-contact particles

Example

using SoftPack

packing = pack(
    rattlers = ContactCount(
        cone_test = true,
    ),
    particles_count = 256,
    seed = 1,
)
count(packing.rattlers)
