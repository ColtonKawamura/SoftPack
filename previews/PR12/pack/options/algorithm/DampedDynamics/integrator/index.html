integrator

Up: DampedDynamics

How the equations of motion are stepped [Samiei2013], [CodeAster]. The three integrators differ in when they evaluate the damping: the centered velocity Verlet solves the damping implicitly and is the most robust; the lagged velocity Verlet uses the previous step's relative velocities and is explicit; the semi-implicit Euler is the simplest explicit scheme.

ChoiceDefaultDamping
:centered_velocity_verletdefaultimplicit, one sparse Cholesky solve per step
:lagged_velocity_verletfrom the previous step's relative velocities
:semi_implicit_eulerexplicit, evaluated at the current velocities

All three are stable at the run's time step, which CollisionTime sets from the stiffest contact. Their stable limits differ, $2/\omega$ for the centered form and $2(\sqrt{1+\zeta^2}-\zeta)/\omega$ for the other two, and the packer warns if the chosen step exceeds the limit of the chosen integrator.

The centered form is the default because it tolerates the largest step and does not introduce a lag in the damping. It costs a sparse Cholesky solve of $M + \tfrac{\Delta t}{2}\Gamma$ every step; with $N = 256$ that is cheaper than the extra steps the explicit forms need.

Example

using SoftPack

packing = pack(
    algorithm = DampedDynamics(
        integrator = :centered_velocity_verlet,
    ),
    particles_count = 64,
    seed = 1,
)
packing.converged
